There is a universal constant \(C{\gt}0\) such that every input hypergraph \(H\) with \(\mathsf{adw}(H)\ge 2\) satisfies

\[ \mathsf{fhw}(H)\le C\lambda (H)\mathsf{adw}(H)\log _2\mathsf{adw}(H). \]

When \(\mathsf{adw}(H){\lt}2\), \(\mathsf{fhw}(H)\le C\lambda (H)\) with the same constant. The Lean statement assumes nonemptiness and does not require connectivity.

Proof ▶

This is the manuscript’s proof. Put \(a=\max \{ 2,\mathsf{adw}(H)\} \). For any \(J=H[W]\) and nonzero \(\gamma \), compactness and continuity give a minimizing fractional half-balanced separator \(x\). Lemma 5.2 gives

\[ \rho _J^*(x)=\beta _{J,1/2}(\gamma ) \le \mathsf{fsep}_{1/2}(H)\le \mathsf{adw}(H)\le a. \]

Structural heredity gives \(\lambda (J)\le \lambda (H)\). Proposition 6.2 supplies an integral \(5/6\)-balanced separator of cost at most \(r=C_0\lambda (H)a\log _2 a\). This follows from monotonicity of \(q\log _2\max \{ 2,q\} \) for \(q\ge 0\). The recursive lemma with \(\eta =5/6\) yields

\[ \mathsf{fhw}(H)\le 14r=14C_0\lambda (H)a\log _2 a. \]

For \(\mathsf{adw}(H)\ge 2\) this is the first case. For \(\mathsf{adw}(H){\lt}2\) it is a constant times \(\lambda (H)\). Lean proves the theorem statement through the alternative route in Section 8. This proof has no separate Lean completion marker.
