8 The Lean route to the same comparison

The checked Lean argument uses a regularized rounding estimate and a separator-plus-one-vertex recursion. Its intermediate inequality differs from Lemma 4.6. The manuscript’s two-separator induction and the sharper specialization in Proposition 6.2 have no one-to-one Lean declarations.

Lemma 8.1 Regularized balanced-separator rounding

There is a universal \(C_0{\gt}0\) such that a fractional \((\gamma ,1/2)\)-balanced \(x\) admits an integral \((\gamma ,5/6)\)-balanced \(S\subseteq \operatorname {supp}x\) with

\[ \rho _H^*(S)\le C_0 \bigl(1+\min \{ d(H),\ln (2+\alpha (H[\operatorname {supp}x]))\} \bigr) (\rho _H^*(x)+1)\ln (2+\rho _H^*(x)). \]
Proof ▶

Use the empty separator when it is balanced. In the other case specialize the proved rounding input at \(\varphi =1/2\) and bound its factors. Lean chooses \(C_0=9(120/\ln 2)\).

Fix \(0{\lt}\eta {\lt}1\) and \(r{\gt}\mathsf{sep}_\eta (H)\), and put \(\Lambda =(r+1)/(1-\eta )\). For \(Z\subseteq W\subseteq V(H)\) with \(\rho _H^*(Z)\le \Lambda \), there is a decomposition of \(H[W]\) with one bag containing \(Z\) and all bags of \(H\)-cover cost at most \(\Lambda +r+1\).

Proof ▶

Induct on \(|W\setminus Z|\). If \(W=Z\), take one bag. Otherwise choose \(u\in W\setminus Z\) and an integral separator \(S\) of cost below \(r\) from an optimal cover of \(Z\), taking \(S=\varnothing \) if \(Z\) is empty. Set \(B=Z\cup S\cup \{ u\} \). For each component \(K\) of \(H[W]-B\), set \(Z_K=B\cap N_{P(H[W])}(K)\) and \(W_K=K\cup Z_K\). Balance gives \(\rho _H^*(Z_K)\le r+1+\eta \Lambda =\Lambda \). The pivot makes \(|K|{\lt}|W\setminus Z|\), so induction applies to each child. Join child roots to the bag \(B\).

  • Paper.Hypergraph.regularized_main_width_comparison

  • Paper.Hypergraph.structuralFactor_le_lambda

There is a universal \(C'{\gt}0\) such that every \(H\) satisfies

\[ \mathsf{fhw}(H)\le C'L(H)(\mathsf{adw}(H)+1)\ln (2+\mathsf{adw}(H)). \]

For nonempty \(H\), \(L(H)\le A(H)\le 4\lambda (H)\). These inequalities, with separate estimates for \(\mathsf{adw}(H)\ge 2\) and \(\mathsf{adw}(H){\lt}2\), give the statement of Theorem 7.1.

Proof ▶

First prove the connected bound and pass to primal components. Replace the component factor by \(4\lambda (H)\). Bound \((a+1)\ln (2+a)\) by a constant times \(a\log _2 a\) for \(a\ge 2\), and by a constant for \(a{\lt}2\). The declaration main_width_comparison applies the proved rounding input to this deduction.

Corollary 8.6 Auxiliary quantitative reverse comparison

Lean declarations

  • Paper.Hypergraph.quantitative_reverse_comparison

There is a universal constant \(c{\gt}0\) such that every finite hypergraph \(H\) satisfies

\[ \mathsf{subw}(H)+1\ge \mathsf{adw}(H)+1 \ge c\frac{\mathsf{fhw}(H)}{L(H)\ln (2+\mathsf{fhw}(H))}. \]
Proof ▶

The parameterized Lean deduction uses the proved rounding theorem. The width hierarchy gives the first inequality and permits replacing \(\ln (2+\mathsf{adw}(H))\) by \(\ln (2+\mathsf{fhw}(H))\) in the upper comparison. Divide by the positive factors and take \(c=C^{-1}\).
